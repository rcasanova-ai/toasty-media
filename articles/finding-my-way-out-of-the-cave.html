Finding My Way Out of the Cave

On losing my social confidence, rediscovering community, and learning to show up again.

I've been thinking a lot lately about human connection, particularly as artificial intelligence makes it increasingly easy for us to work, create, communicate, and even run businesses without having to interact with another human being.

There's something remarkable about that freedom. I can sit at home, develop ideas, build applications, research virtually anything, and accomplish things that would have been unimaginable to me just a few years ago. For someone who has always had more ideas than resources to execute them, it's an extraordinary time to be alive.

But I've also been wondering whether all this convenience comes with a rather uncomfortable side effect. When technology makes it possible to do almost everything without leaving the house, what happens when leaving the house is something you're already struggling to do?

It's a question I've been confronting in my own life, and one that feels particularly relevant as I write more about community, relationships, and the importance of putting people back into business.

Because if I'm going to encourage people to share their stories, I suppose I should be willing to share a little more of mine.

Finding my way back out of the cave

For much of my life, I was an incredibly social person. Back in Canada, I was involved in organizing events, bringing people together, making introductions, and generally finding any excuse to be around people. I ran multiple community organizations and businesses in Edmonton for years, and connecting people was something I genuinely enjoyed. I was comfortable walking into a room full of strangers, starting conversations, and finding common ground with just about anyone.

Somewhere along the way, that changed.

I’ve struggled with my mental health, and over time I found myself becoming increasingly withdrawn. The person who used to thrive on social interaction gradually became someone who was much more comfortable being alone. And the longer I spent in that environment, the harder it became to leave it.

When I moved to Southeast Asia, I initially found myself getting back into the rhythm of things. I was attending events, meeting people, exploring different communities, and enjoying that feeling of being part of something again. For a while, I was showing up at practically every event I could find.

But the struggle didn’t disappear.

As I write this, I’m sitting in what I’ve come to think of as my cave. I’ve found myself withdrawing again, and even the thought of going out and engaging with people can sometimes feel exhausting. It’s a strange experience when you remember being someone who could spend an entire evening talking to strangers and leave feeling energized.

These days, I feel like I need one of those T-shirts with a social battery indicator on the front so people know how much conversation I have left in me.

The irony isn’t lost on me. Here I am writing about the importance of human connection, community, and building relationships, while simultaneously struggling to get myself out the door to participate in those very things.

I’ve been engaging more recently with Thailand’s Superteam community, @solana @superteamTH and it’s helping. They have encouraged me to engage, show up to the buildstation they recently opened and even enter the @colosseum. There are some genuinely talented people doing interesting things, and being around that energy reminds me of what I used to enjoy so much about bringing people together. I’m starting to find my rhythm again, although some days are considerably easier than others.

And to be clear, the challenge has nothing to do with the people or the quality of the community. That’s there. The struggle is mine, and I’m still figuring out how to navigate it.

I don’t share this because I’m looking for sympathy or because I think there’s something particularly remarkable about my experience. I share it because it’s part of my story, and if I’m going to encourage people to be more open about their journeys, it would be pretty hypocritical to present only the polished parts of my own.

I’m still building. I’m still creating. I’ve got several projects I’m passionate about, ideas I’m excited to bring into the world, and plenty of ambitions that probably sound a little ridiculous to anyone who hasn’t spent time inside my head.

But I’m also trying to reconnect with the part of myself that genuinely enjoyed being around people, sharing ideas, and helping others accomplish things.

Maybe building in public can be part of that process too.

Where I think this is heading

I don’t have all of this figured out. I’m still finding my groove, still learning how to balance the solitude I need with the human connection I miss, and still working through some of the things that made me withdraw in the first place.

But I know I have something to contribute, and I know there are people out there worth building alongside.

So I’m going to start sharing more of what I’m working on, the ideas behind it, the things that go wrong, and hopefully a few things that go right. I’m going to tell more of my story, introduce the projects I’ve been quietly developing, and make a more deliberate effort to reconnect with the communities around me.

I’ve spent enough time building things behind closed doors. And who knows? I might even take the world by storm.
