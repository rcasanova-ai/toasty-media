Santati Research · Infrastructure

What does it take to power a data centre?

Every data centre needs electricity, cooling, land and connections to the outside world. Understanding where those resources come from is the starting point for understanding the real cost of AI infrastructure.

01 · Electricity

How much power does it use?

Compare the facility's stated IT load in megawatts with total facility demand where disclosed. Distinguish electricity connected to the grid from electricity actually consumed.

02 · Energy mix

Where does the energy come from?

Identify grid supply, renewable contracts, nuclear, coal, gas and on-site generation. A renewable purchase agreement does not necessarily mean the facility runs on renewable electricity every hour.

03 · Fuel and generation

Is a power plant being built for it?

Track gas turbines, generators, substations, transmission lines and fuel pipelines. Where natural gas is used, identify whether it is pipeline gas or sourced through an LNG supply chain, when publicly verifiable.

04 · Water and cooling

Where does the cooling water come from?

Track drinking-water supplies, reclaimed water, groundwater and cooling technologies. Separate water withdrawn from water consumed, and note how figures vary with weather and operating conditions.

05 · Land and people

What changes around the facility?

Look at land purchases, zoning, residential displacement claims, construction traffic, emissions and noise. Document affected communities with primary records rather than repeating unverified claims.

06 · Costs and jobs

Who pays, and who benefits?

Examine utility upgrades, taxpayer incentives, electricity rate impacts, construction employment and long-term operations jobs. Keep forecasts and confirmed outcomes separate.

Case study · Memphis and Southaven

How xAI powers Colossus

The operator says its original Memphis facility uses electricity supplied by Memphis Light, Gas and Water via dedicated substations, with battery storage and natural gas turbines for backup. At the Southaven, Mississippi expansion, the operator reported on July 30, 2026 that it was constructing a dedicated 1.2 GW gas-fired power plant using 41 permitted permanent turbines. It described plans to remove temporary mobile turbines by July 2027. Those are company statements about plans and infrastructure, not independently measured current generation.

Reuters reported on July 14, 2026 that temporary turbine operations raised air-pollution and permitting concerns for surrounding communities. The operator disputes allegations of noncompliance. The precise gas supply chain, including any LNG component, and neighborhood purchase claims should be verified from primary records before being stated as facts.

Sources: Operator facility information · Operator July 2026 update · Reuters reporting.

Why power generation matters

The International Energy Agency projects global data centre electricity use growing from about 485 TWh in 2025 to around 950 TWh by 2030. Renewable electricity, gas, coal and nuclear all contribute to supply. Every facility profile should distinguish the local electricity mix, contracted supply, onsite generation, fuel origin and actual operating data. IEA source.

How we verify a facility

Each future facility profile should name its operator, location, operating status, disclosed capacity, energy arrangements, water source, local infrastructure, relevant permits, community impacts, source links and last verification date. Unknown or contested figures should remain explicitly marked as such.

This is an overview of the research framework, not a claim that every facility has been independently audited. For the wider research program, visit Santati Research.
